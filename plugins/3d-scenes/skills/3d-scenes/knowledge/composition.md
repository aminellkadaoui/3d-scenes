## 1. The one rule: every frame has a job and one place to look
Composition is controlling *where the eye goes, in what order, and what it feels*. Grids and named techniques are tools to achieve that. Staging principle: "make your idea obvious" and reveal information in sequence [JQT9sT1YuAI@09:45–10:00].
Before placing anything, write: **Shot job** (e.g. "show the product is small and premium"), **focal point** (one), **secondary** (≤1), **feeling** (calm/power/urgency), **overlay plan** (where text goes).

## 2. Visual hierarchy & single focal point
The eye is pulled, roughly in this order, by: highest local value contrast → faces/eyes and text → saturated color on neutral ground ("pop of color" [HBWYieo5RmU@07:45]) → sharp vs blurred → detail vs plain → the odd one out ("breaking the pattern" [HBWYieo5RmU@05:15]) → lines pointing to it → motion (in video, motion beats everything).
Make sure all of those agree on the subject. If the brightest edge is a window and the subject is mid-grey, the window wins. See `techniques/composition-focal-point-value-contrast.md`.
Keep 1–3 elements on screen; a frame is read like a thumbnail — if it costs effort, viewers leave [skool-rr-cinematic-composition@02:29–02:53]. Four focus tools that should agree: quantity, color, light, DoF [skool-rr-cinematic-composition@02:20–05:23]. Selective color (grey world, colored subject) is the documentary version of pop of color → `techniques/composition-selective-color-isolation.md` [skool-rr-cinematic-composition@03:13].

## 3. Placement grids: tools, not laws
- **Rule of thirds** [HBWYieo5RmU@00:30], **phi / golden ratio** [00:15], **golden triangle** (subject at diagonal intersection, or elements along diagonals) [01:45]. Practically all say "off-center, roughly 1/3–0.38 from an edge"; the golden-ratio-in-nature claim is oversold — don't pick phi over thirds for mystical reasons.
- **Center/symmetry** is a valid choice [00:30–00:45]: hero products, logos, architecture, confrontation, formal brand moments. If symmetric, be *exactly* symmetric (camera on axis, Shift X/Y not tilt); near-symmetry looks like a mistake. Center framing only works if distractions outside the center are removed (blur, plain surrounding mass like water) [skool-rr-cinematic-composition@01:48–02:08].
- Blender: Camera Properties ▸ Viewport Display ▸ **Composition Guides** (Center, Center Diagonal, Thirds, Golden Ratio, Golden Triangle A/B, Harmonious Triangle A/B) and **Safe Areas**. Turn on the guide that matches the intended design, look through camera (`Numpad 0`), lock camera to view only for blocking.
- Slop sign: *every* shot centered at the same size, or every shot obsessively on a thirds intersection with no reason.

## 4. Lines and shapes
- **Leading lines** to subject [02:15]; **implied lines** from gaze/arrangement [02:15–02:30]; **converging lines** for depth/journey [02:30]; **diagonals** = motion (parallel) or tension (opposed) [02:45]; **horizontals** calm, **verticals** strength [02:45–03:00]; **S-curves** elegance/flow, **C-curves** softness [03:00]; **shapes**: triangles stability/tension, circles harmony, rectangles order [03:15].
- 3D application: you *build* the lines. Floor seams, table edges, cables, light streaks, rows of objects, shadow edges, architectural lines — aim them at the subject. Avoid lines that lead out of frame or crash into frame corners.
- Avoid **tangents**: edges that just touch (product edge kissing the horizon line, object touching frame edge) create visual tension that reads as error. Overlap clearly or separate clearly.

## 5. Framing within the frame
Doorways, windows, arches, shelves, foliage, foreground silhouettes enclose the subject [01:30–01:45]. In 3D this is cheap: a dark, slightly defocused foreground frame on 1–2 edges. Keep it darker/lower-contrast than the subject.

## 6. Negative space & fill the frame
- **Fill the frame** = intimacy, detail, intensity [01:00–01:15]. **Negative space** = scale, isolation, calm, luxury [01:15–01:30].
- In VSL, negative space is also **text space**. Plan it (§11).

## 7. Depth: foreground / midground / background, overlap, scale cues
- Three planes + overlap = depth [03:45–04:00]; **scale contrast** tiny vs huge [04:00]; **leading foreground interest** pulls the eye in [04:15].
- Depth cues to stack: overlap, relative size, perspective lines, value/contrast falloff with distance (aerial perspective: background lighter/lower contrast or darker), focus, and in motion, parallax.
- Scale cues: include something of known size (a person, door, cup, chair) when scale matters; without it a "huge" building or "tiny" device reads ambiguous. Correct real-world scale in Blender also makes DoF and lighting read correctly.
- Card: `techniques/composition-depth-layers-fg-mg-bg.md`.

## 8. Balance & visual weight
- Balance ≠ mirroring [04:30]. Weight rises with brightness, saturation, size, detail/busyness, isolation, and distance from center (edge elements weigh more; "even a little bit of a tree at the edge can balance the scene") [04:45].
- Asymmetric balance: large dark mass on one side, small bright accent on the other [04:45].
- **Rule of odds** [01:00]: groups of 3/5 feel more natural than pairs; use for prop clusters, vary scale and spacing.
- **Repetition / rhythm** [05:00] then **break the pattern** with the hero [05:15]: a row of grey bottles, one with the brand color.
- **Dynamic tension** [05:30–05:45]: opposing diagonals, cramped lead room, near-collisions — use only for tension beats (problem/pain sections of a VSL).

## 9. Contrast of value, color, sharpness to guide the eye
- **Chiaroscuro** (strong local light/dark) [06:00]; **high key / low key** overall brightness [06:15]; **silhouette** (backlit dark subject) [06:30].
- Color schemes [06:45–07:45]: complementary (energy), analogous (harmony), triadic (vibrant, hard to control), warm/cool contrast (warm subject vs cool ambience advances the subject), color dominance / monochrome (focus on shape/light), pop of color (one saturated accent on neutral = strongest focal tool). The video uses the RYB painter's wheel; in digital RGB complements differ slightly — use as rough guide.
- Sharpness: subject sharpest; DoF or simpler, less textured backgrounds.
- **Figure–ground:** subject never the same value/hue as what is directly behind it; dark clothes → lighter wall, dark background → lighter/warmer subject [skool-rr-cinematic-composition@09:44].
- **Juxtaposition** (old/new, cheap/premium) [08:00] — perfect for before/after VSL shots. **Storytelling composition** [08:15], **minimalism** — remove anything that doesn't serve the job [08:15].

## 10. Eye level, horizon placement, point of view
- Camera height = meaning: eye level natural, low = power, high = vulnerability/overview [03:30–03:45]. Details in camera-lenses.md §4.
- Horizon: on upper or lower third rather than center unless symmetry is intended; keep it level; don't let it cut through the subject's head/top edge (tangent). Move horizon with lens Shift Y to keep verticals straight.

## 12. Reading composition from a render (diagnostics)
1. **Squint/blur test**: blur the frame heavily (or squint). The first blob you see must be the subject.
2. **Grayscale value check**: desaturate. Is there a clear light/dark structure (2–4 big value groups)? Is the strongest contrast at the subject? Uniform mid-grey = flat, slop-prone.
3. **Thumbnail test**: shrink to ~160 px wide (YouTube/feed size). Can you tell what it is and where to look?
4. **Flip test**: mirror horizontally — awkward balance and tangents jump out to fresh eyes.
5. **Edge walk**: trace the 4 frame edges: half-cut objects, bright spots, lines exiting at corners, tangents.
6. **Overlay test**: dummy text/caption plate on top at final aspect ratio.
7. **5-second test**: show someone for 5 s, ask what they saw first and what the shot is about.

## 14. Slop signs (composition)
- Subject dead center at medium size in every shot; same height, same lens.
- Single depth plane; object floats on a gradient with no contact shadow, no foreground, no context.
- Everything equally lit, equally sharp, equally saturated → no hierarchy.
- Random props scattered evenly ("clutter by spec"), even counts, uniform spacing.
- Horizon through the middle, slightly tilted.
- Text added later over the busiest part of the frame.
- Stacking many "techniques" (symmetry + leading lines + framing + pop of color + Dutch angle) with no shot job.

## 15. Composition QA checklist (score each 1–5)
- [ ] Shot job + single focal point stated, and the render agrees (squint + grayscale tests pass).
- [ ] Placement chosen deliberately (thirds/center/golden) — and consistent with sequence variety (wide/medium/close).
- [ ] Depth: ≥2 planes (3 unless minimal studio), overlap present, background lower contrast than subject.
- [ ] Lines lead to, not away from, the subject; no tangents; clean frame edges.
- [ ] Balance intentional (symmetric exact or asymmetric balanced); no accidental heavy edge element.
- [ ] Camera height/angle motivated; horizon level and on a third (or intentionally centered).
- [ ] Color: accent reserved for subject; palette scheme chosen.
- [ ] Lead room in direction of gaze/motion.
- [ ] Overlay space reserved and clean for the full shot duration, in each delivery aspect ratio.
- [ ] Thumbnail (160 px) readable; 5-second test passes.
- [ ] Composition holds on first, middle and last frame of a moving shot.
