# Wide to establish, close-up to reveal (shot pairing)

**Principle:** A wide shot answers "where are we / what is going on" (context); a close-up delivers emotion, reaction or the detail that changes the meaning. A close-up without its wide is unreadable; a wide without a close stays generic [skool-rr-cinematic-composition@06:08–06:53]. Examples: ATM wide → close shows he is stealing it; mansion wide (rich) → close on gold + drugs (rich illegally) [07:34–08:20].

**Steps / settings (starting points):**
1. In the shot list, write each beat as a pair: WIDE (context) → CLOSE (the information/emotion).
2. Wide: 18–28 mm, often slightly high (looking down) [08:42]; show the whole space and the subject small in it; one dominant element still.
3. Close: 50–85 mm, straight-on / eye level [08:42]; face, hands or the key object; shallow DoF.
4. The close must add new information (not just a bigger version of the wide).
5. Keep the same light direction and screen direction between the pair (no 180° jump).
6. Authority/power character: low angle; vulnerable: high angle [09:02].

**Use when:** the start of every scene/location; any reveal beat.
**Avoid when:** the audience already knows the place (repeating the wide each time slows the pace) — then a medium can re-establish.

**Failure signs:** sequence opens on a tight shot and the viewer can't place it; every shot the same size and lens; close-up that reveals nothing new; light/screen direction flips between wide and close.
**Cost:** one extra camera + shot; cheap.
**Conflicts with other sources:** none. "Most wides are high angle" is a tendency, not a rule.
**Test to validate:** show the close alone vs wide→close to 2–3 viewers; ask "where is he and what's happening?".
