# Foreground / midground / background layering with overlap

**Principle:** Depth is perceived from overlap, relative size, parallax in motion, value/contrast falloff with distance (aerial perspective) and focus. A subject alone on a plane in a void has one depth plane = flat "3D render" look. Three planes + overlap make the frame feel spatial [HBWYieo5RmU@03:45], [HBWYieo5RmU@04:00]; a foreground element also guides the eye in [HBWYieo5RmU@04:15] and can frame the subject [HBWYieo5RmU@01:30].

**Steps / settings (starting points):**
1. Decide the midground = subject plane.
2. Foreground: an object partially entering the frame edge (shelf edge, plant leaves, door frame, glass, out-of-focus bokeh lights) covering ~5–20 % of frame, darker or lower-contrast than subject; slightly defocused if DoF used.
3. Background: context with lower contrast and less detail than subject (darker or lighter, less saturated; volume/mist to lift).
4. Overlap: at least one element overlaps another plane (foreground over subject's edge, subject over background object).
5. In motion: lateral camera moves (slide/truck) reveal parallax only if planes exist — foreground moves fastest.
6. Check with camera view at the final aspect ratio.

**Use when:** almost every non-graphic shot; environments, interiors, product-in-context.
**Avoid when:** clean studio product on seamless (deliberate minimalism — then depth comes from lighting gradient + shadow), pure infographic shots; foreground covering text-safe area.

**Failure signs:** foreground brighter/sharper than subject (steals focus); foreground intersecting subject's silhouette awkwardly (tangents); foreground object recognizable but meaningless; background as detailed and contrasty as subject; everything at same distance.
**Cost:** a few more assets; DoF may add sampling.
**Conflicts with other sources:** none.
**Test to validate:** same shot A subject+backdrop only, B with defocused foreground element and darker background; 5-second "where did you look / how deep is it" test.
